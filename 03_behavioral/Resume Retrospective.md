1. **Looking back, what capabilities did working at Amazon actually give you that you're only appreciating now during your job search?**
	1. Writing code strategically, instead of just writing code that works.
	2. Thoroughly documenting code.
	3. Using consistent formatting when writing code (for improved readability).
	4. Writing thorough design documents.
	5. Giving and receiving feedback on work product.
	6. Thoroughly documenting failures and writing SOPs.
	7. Responding to and mitigating emergencies in production.
2. **What did losing your job reveal about your own assumptions regarding careers, employability, or professional development?**
	1. Careers don’t last forever. I was originally planning to just stay at Amazon for as long as I could.
	2. Keeping your job isn’t always about doing your best and meeting expectations.
	3. Professional development is always important, even while you’re employed, since you never when/if you’re going to need to look for a new job.
3. **What did the bootcamp genuinely add to your capabilities beyond technical knowledge? Did it change how you think, work, document, or approach problems?**
	1. Working with multiple pieces of technology to build software.
	2. Working in teams and collaborating.
	3. Explicitly documenting code, so that its function and purpose is clear, even to someone who has never read it before.
	4. Solving problems by breaking them down into manageable components, instead of trying to solve the whole problem all at once.
4. **When you build personal projects now, what are you trying to demonstrate? Technical ability? Judgment? Initiative? Something else?**
	1. When I build personal projects, I’m mostly trying to demonstrate technical ability and initiative.
5. **You've put a lot of effort into documenting your work on GitHub. Has that changed how you think about creating work that other people can understand and evaluate?**
	1. I document my work on GitHub, including the steps I took and challenges I faced along the way, so that I have reference to go back to if I face similar problems in the future.
	2. I also document my work on GitHub out of habit, as all of my software-related work at Amazon had to be peer reviewed and documented GitHub.
6. **Has the search itself changed what you think employers are actually evaluating?**
	1. Yes, I don’t have a lot of actual experience interviewing given that my career trajectory has been based more on merit and work ethic than interviewing skills. The search has taught me that hiring managers are looking for people who don’t just have the skills needed to do the job, but also have the ability to effectively communicate and convince someone that they’re the right fit for the job.
7. **Do you feel like you're becoming qualified for one specific role, or do you feel like you're building capabilities that could transfer across several adjacent roles over time?**
	1. Given that most of my work at Data Engineering Academy has involved writing code and learning new languages such as SQL and Python, I feel like I’m becoming more qualified as a software engineer who specializes in building data pipelines. 
	2. Building the final dashboards that consume data from these pipelines is also teaching me how to analyze and present data.
8. **If someone looked only at everything you've done since leaving Amazon—the bootcamp, projects, documentation, applications—what capability do you hope they'd conclude you've been building?**
	1. Working with data and building reliable data pipelines.
